Irish Soda Bread

Makes 1 loaf (8 slices)  ·  Prep 10 min  ·  Cook 40 min

Four ingredients, no yeast, no waiting — all comfort. This is the bread that never lets an Irish kitchen down: stirred together in minutes, shaped into a round, and cut with a deep cross on top to bless the bread and let the fairies out. Serve it the only way that matters — warm, with cold butter and a strong cup of tea. That's the whole sermon.

Ingredients

  • 450 g (3½ cups) plain flour, plus extra for dusting
  • 1 tsp fine sea salt
  • 1 tsp baking soda (bicarbonate of soda)
  • About 400 ml (1⅔ cups) buttermilk

Method

  1. Heat the oven to 230°C / 450°F (210°C fan). Lightly flour a baking tray.
  2. Whisk the flour, salt and baking soda together in a big bowl.
  3. Pour in the buttermilk and mix with a fork or your hand to a soft, sticky dough. Use light hands — overworking makes the bread tough.
  4. Turn onto a floured surface, shape into a round about 4 cm (1½ in) thick, and set it on the tray.
  5. Cut a deep cross in the top, almost through to the base — to bless the bread, and let the fairies out.
  6. Bake for 30–45 minutes, until deep golden. Tap the bottom: it should sound hollow.
  7. Cool on a rack for at least 15 minutes before slicing. Best eaten the day it is baked.

From the pantry

  • Buttermilk powder — for soda bread on demand
  • Strong bread flour
  • A cast iron skillet — soda bread loves one
