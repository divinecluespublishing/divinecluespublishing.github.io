In Tara's Kitchen

Colcannon

Serves 6  ·  Prep 15 min  ·  Cook 25 min

Mashed potatoes, but make them Irish: potatoes whipped with kale, scallions and an indecent amount of butter, with a well melted into the middle of every serving — because that's not a garnish, that's the architecture. The old Halloween tradition hid a ring, a coin and a thimble in the colcannon to tell your fortune. I hide nothing in mine except more butter.

Ingredients

  • 1 kg (2¼ lb) floury potatoes, peeled and quartered
  • 200 g (7 oz) kale or green cabbage, tough stalks removed, finely shredded
  • 100 g (7 tbsp) butter, plus extra for the well
  • 100 ml (scant ½ cup) milk, heated
  • 4 scallions (spring onions), finely chopped
  • Fine sea salt and black pepper

Method

  1. Boil the potatoes in salted water for 20–25 minutes, until completely tender.
  2. Meanwhile, blanch the kale or cabbage in boiling water for 3–4 minutes; drain well and squeeze dry.
  3. Drain the potatoes, return them to the hot pot, and let them steam dry for a minute.
  4. Mash with the butter and hot milk until smooth and fluffy.
  5. Fold through the kale and scallions, season generously, and taste.
  6. Serve in warm bowls, each mound with a well in the centre holding a melting knob of butter. Dip every forkful through the butter as you go.

From the pantry

  • A potato ricer — for lump-free mash
  • Flaky sea salt
  • A cast iron skillet — for serving it sizzling
