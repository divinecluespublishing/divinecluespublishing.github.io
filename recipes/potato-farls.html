Potato Farls

Serves 4 (4 farls)  ·  Prep 10 min  ·  Cook 15 min

Yesterday's mash, quartered and griddled: the farl is the best second act in Irish cooking. Farl means a quarter, and that's the whole method — roll out the round, cut it in four, and griddle them golden. Fried in the pan drippings if you're doing it properly, under a fried egg if you're doing it my way. Nothing wasted, everything crisp.

Ingredients

  • 500 g (1 lb) mashed potato — leftover is perfect, warm or cold
  • 100–125 g (¾–1 cup) plain flour, plus extra for dusting
  • ½ tsp fine sea salt
  • 25 g (2 tbsp) butter, melted (optional, but good)

Method

  1. Work the flour, salt and melted butter (if using) into the mashed potato to make a soft, pliable dough — add flour a spoonful at a time; you want it soft, not stiff.
  2. On a well-floured surface, roll or pat the dough into a round about 8 mm (⅓ in) thick.
  3. Cut the round into four quarters — the farls.
  4. Heat a griddle or heavy frying pan over medium heat and dust it lightly with flour.
  5. Griddle the farls for 3–4 minutes a side, until golden with darker patches.
  6. Serve hot, split and buttered, or under a fried egg.

From the pantry

  • A cast iron griddle pan
  • A wooden rolling pin
  • Flour for dusting — keep a bag by the stove
