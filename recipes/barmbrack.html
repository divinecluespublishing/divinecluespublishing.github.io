Barmbrack

Makes 1 loaf (about 10 slices)  ·  Prep 15 min  ·  Cook 1 hr

A tea-soaked fruit loaf with a fortune baked inside. The fruit drinks strong black tea overnight and comes out plump and dark, folded into a soft, spiced loaf. Traditionally a ring is baked into the brack — find it in your slice and love is coming for you. I write romance; I am professionally obliged to respect the ring.

Ingredients

  • 350 g (12 oz) mixed dried fruit — raisins, sultanas and currants
  • 300 ml (1¼ cups) strong hot black tea
  • 225 g (1¾ cups) self-raising flour
  • 175 g (¾ cup, packed) soft brown sugar
  • 1 tsp mixed spice
  • 1 egg, beaten
  • A plain ring, wrapped in parchment (optional, traditional at Halloween)

Method

  1. The night before: put the dried fruit in a bowl, pour over the hot tea, cover, and leave to soak overnight.
  2. Heat the oven to 170°C / 325°F (150°C fan). Line a 900 g (2 lb) loaf tin with parchment.
  3. Stir the flour, sugar and mixed spice into the soaked fruit and its tea, then stir in the beaten egg to make a soft batter.
  4. If using the ring, fold it in now. Scrape the batter into the tin and level the top.
  5. Bake for about 1 hour, until risen, deep golden, and a skewer pushed into the centre comes out clean.
  6. Cool in the tin for 10 minutes, then turn out. Serve sliced, thickly buttered, with strong tea. Keeps for days in a tin — if it lasts.
