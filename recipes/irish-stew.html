Traditional Irish Stew

Serves 6  ·  Prep 20 min  ·  Cook 2 hr

Lamb, potatoes, onions, carrots, thyme — and time. A stew can't be rushed, and neither can the good things. There are no thickeners and no shortcuts here: the floury potatoes break down as it simmers and do the thickening themselves, the way they have for two hundred years.

Ingredients

  • 1 kg (2¼ lb) lamb shoulder or neck, cut into large chunks
  • 1 kg (2¼ lb) potatoes — half floury, half waxy — peeled and cut into large chunks
  • 500 g (1 lb) onions, thickly sliced
  • 400 g (14 oz) carrots, cut into chunks
  • 2 sprigs of fresh thyme (or 1 tsp dried)
  • About 1 litre (4 cups) water or light lamb or chicken stock
  • Fine sea salt and black pepper
  • Chopped fresh parsley, to serve

Method

  1. Layer the lamb, onions, potatoes and carrots in a heavy pot, seasoning each layer with salt and pepper and scattering the thyme through.
  2. Pour in just enough water or stock to barely cover. Bring slowly to a simmer and skim off any foam.
  3. Cover with a tight lid and cook at the gentlest simmer for 1½–2 hours, until the lamb is tender and the floury potatoes have broken down to thicken the broth.
  4. Do not stir much — a shake of the pot now and then is plenty. Taste and adjust the seasoning.
  5. Rest off the heat for 10 minutes, scatter with parsley, and serve in warm bowls.

From the pantry

  • A cast iron Dutch oven — built for slow stews
  • Dried thyme
  • Flaky sea salt
